I’m a computational scientist at the [UKAEA](https://www.ukaea.org), the United Kingdom’s research centre for nuclear fusion. I combine advanced mathematics (numerical analysis, nonlinear programming, and mathematical modelling) with high-performance computing to run faster simulations of the plasma inside fusion reactors (tokamaks).

My research is on parallel-in-time methods for chaotic systems. Ordinary time-stepping is sequential, so a longer simulation can’t be sped up just by adding processors. Parallel-in-time methods compute many points in time at once, but they struggle where small errors grow exponentially. I work on a moving-window algorithm with finite-time convergence guarantees, aimed at tokamak simulations.

During my time at Oxford’s [Centre for Doctoral Training in Industrially Focused Mathematical Modelling](https://www.maths.ox.ac.uk/study-here/postgraduate-study/industrially-focused-mathematical-modelling-epsrc-cdt), I worked with companies on industrial problems: the risk of satellite collisions for Transfinite Systems, sending and detecting signals on the same channel at once for Dstl (the research arm of the UK Ministry of Defence), and how accurately crowds are counted at airports for CrowdVision.

Publications, talks, and the full record are in my [CV](cv.html).
